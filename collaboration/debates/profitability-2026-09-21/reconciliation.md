# Reconciliation — how do we make this profitable?

21 September 2026. Claude's opening was sealed and committed before
Codex was asked. Codex answered without sight of it.

## Codex found an error in Claude's position

Claude claimed the exchange move "takes us from losing to roughly
break-even". **Wrong.** Commission applies to net winnings, so the
effective price is `1 + (odds − 1) × (1 − commission)` and must be
applied before computing EV. Recomputed on the same three selections:

| | Mean EV per bet |
|---|---|
| Bookmaker (baseline) | −7.80% |
| Exchange at 2% commission | **−2.68%** |
| Exchange at 5% commission | **−4.64%** |

Codex's figures were −2.7% and −4.6%; independent recomputation gives
−2.68% and −4.64%. It is right and the correction is material: the
venue change recovers 5.1pp at best and **leaves the method losing**.

**This raises the bar.** To break even on the exchange we need
demonstrated edge of at least **2.7pp against the closing line**, not
zero. Claude's framing implied the hard part was already solved by
changing venue. It is not.

Codex also predicted, without seeing it, that Claude's likely mistake
would be *"believing exchange pricing plus richer AI analysis closes the
remaining gap"*. That is precisely the error it then found. Recorded
because it is the strongest vindication of the blind protocol so far.

## Where they agree

- **No demonstrated edge exists today.** Both say so unprompted.
- **The 72.5% selection rate is the disease.** Stop treating the 3pp
  rule as evidence of value.
- **Do not expand leagues.** Lower-division margins make it worse.
- **Do not read £35.09 as validation.**
- **Keep staking suspended**, and keep execution outside the desk.
- The one *plausible* mechanism is the **team-news window**: correct
  information incorporated into our price before it is fully in an
  executable exchange price. Both independently named it, and both
  independently said it is conjecture, not a finding.

## Where they differ

**On what the team-news window is worth.** Claude called it "the one
place where two AIs have a structural advantage over a human". Codex
rejected that framing: the advantage is only *throughput* — extracting
and reconciling timestamped availability data from many unstructured
sources — and *"nothing here proves that workflow beats a competent
human, much less the market."* Codex is right that Claude overstated it.

**On what 60 selections proves.** Claude treated the decision point as a
verdict. Codex: *"a useful spending limit, not a certification
threshold"*, and correlated selections reduce its effective information
further. Adopted — section 15's decision point stands as a stopping
rule, but a positive result is a screening outcome, not a licence.

**On the scoreboard.** Codex adds that median CLV alone can hide
damaging tails, and that we should report commission-adjusted expected
return, mean and uncertainty alongside it. Adopted.

**On model calls.** Codex: stop spending them on arithmetic and
reporting — scripts should compute probabilities, commission, CLV and
audits; reserve the models for genuinely ambiguous information.
Adopted.

## Codex's probability

**30%** that a frozen, prospective 60-selection Premier League exchange
run shows positive median CLV. Its probability of *sustainable profit*
after commission and friction is explicitly lower, with the caveat:
*"This may simply not be profitably salvageable. The next experiment
should earn continued investment, not presume it."*

Claude did not state a number in its sealed opening. Asked afterwards
and therefore not blind: **20%** for the same question, and lower again
for sustainable profit. The two are close enough that the disagreement
is not where the decision lies.

## What changes as a result

1. Section 16 corrected: the exchange leaves us at −2.7%/−4.6%, not
   break-even. Published on the public page too.
2. Section 15 gains: report commission-adjusted EV, mean and
   uncertainty alongside median CLV; a positive result is screening,
   not certification.
3. Selection rate capped — Claude proposed under 5%, unopposed.
4. The team-news window is worth **one bounded, paper-only,
   pre-registered experiment**, compared against both Dixon-Coles alone
   and a contemporaneous market baseline on identical fixtures. Not a
   strategy. Not yet a belief.
